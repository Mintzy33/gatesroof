## The Short Answer: Who Handles a Hail-Damage Roof Insurance Claim in Denver?

A hail-damage roof insurance claim in Denver is handled by **four parties working together**, and knowing who does what is the difference between a full payout and a shortchanged one:

1. **You, the homeowner** — you own the claim. You file it, you choose your contractor, and you pay your deductible.
2. **Your insurance company and its adjuster** — they inspect the damage, write the scope of loss, and pay the claim minus your deductible.
3. **A licensed local roofing contractor with insurance-restoration experience** — they inspect your roof, document the hail damage, meet your adjuster on the roof, file supplements for anything missed, and complete the work to code. **This is the role Gates Enterprises plays for Denver-area homeowners.**
4. **A public adjuster or property-damage attorney** — only needed if your insurer unreasonably underpays or denies a valid claim.

For most Denver homeowners, the practical first call is a **local insurance-restoration roofing contractor** — not the insurance company, and not an attorney. A good one runs the claim with you from the first inspection to the final depreciation check, at no cost beyond your deductible. ## Step-by-Step: How a Denver Hail Claim Actually Works

**1. Document the storm immediately.** Photograph the roof from the ground, plus gutters, downspouts, siding, screens, the AC unit, and any dented vehicles — adjusters use these collateral indicators to confirm a hail event. Save the date and any local weather alerts.

**2. Get an independent roof inspection *before* the adjuster.** Most homeowners skip this and it costs them. A storm-experienced roofer documents every slope so you have a benchmark to compare against the adjuster's findings. Gates Enterprises offers free storm-damage inspections across the Denver metro [LINK: free-inspection].

**3. File the claim with your insurer.** Report the facts — a hailstorm struck, you've observed roof and property damage — get your claim number, and ask whether your roofing contractor may be present for the adjuster inspection. The answer should be yes.

**4. Be present for the adjuster — with your contractor.** When your roofer walks the roof alongside the adjuster, damage gets documented in real time and there are fewer disputes later. Gates includes this on every claim [LINK: insurance-claims].

**5. Review the scope of loss line by line.** Compare the insurer's estimate to your contractor's. Common omissions: ridge cap, starter strip, drip edge, ice-and-water shield, full slopes, and related gutter/siding damage. A lower insurance estimate is not a denial — it's the starting point.

**6. Know your ACV vs. RCV.** This is where Denver homeowners lose the most money. See the table below.

**7. Let your contractor file the supplement.** If the real cost exceeds the insurer's scope, your contractor submits a documented supplement (priced in Xactimate, the software most carriers use). Supplements are routine in Colorado, not confrontational.

**8. Recover your depreciation.** On an RCV policy, the insurer holds back "recoverable depreciation" until the work is done. Submit the final invoice and request the release — this step is the one most often forgotten, and it's frequently thousands of dollars.

## ACV vs. RCV: The Number That Decides Your Payout

| | Actual Cash Value (ACV) | Replacement Cost Value (RCV) |
|---|---|---|
| **What it pays** | Depreciated value of the roof (age + condition) | Full cost to replace with like materials at today's prices |
| **A 15-yr-old roof** | Pays a fraction of replacement cost | Pays full replacement (in two stages) |
| **How you're paid** | One reduced check | ACV up front, then the depreciation holdback released after the work is done |
| **Most CO policies today** | Less common | **Most common — but confirm yours** |

If you have an RCV policy, **don't forget to claim the recoverable depreciation** after the roof is replaced.

## Your Deductible in Denver

Most Colorado homeowner policies carry a **wind/hail deductible stated as a percentage of your home's insured value**, not a flat dollar amount:

| Dwelling coverage | 1% deductible | 2% deductible |
|---|---|---|
| $400,000 | $4,000 | $8,000 |
| $500,000 | $5,000 | $10,000 |
| $600,000 | $6,000 | $12,000 |

You pay the deductible regardless of claim size. **Be very cautious of any contractor who offers to waive, cover, or "eat" your deductible** — see the Colorado law section below.

## The Colorado Laws That Protect Your Claim

These statutes are what separate an informed Denver homeowner from an easy target. (General information, not legal advice — for a specific dispute, talk to a Colorado attorney.)

- **A contractor cannot pay, waive, or rebate your deductible.** Under Colorado's Residential Roofing law (C.R.S. § 6-22-105), paying or waiving a homeowner's insurance deductible is illegal — a Class 2 misdemeanor. A contractor who offers it is breaking the law and signaling exactly how they'll treat your roof.
- **You get a written contract and a 72-hour right to rescind.** Under C.R.S. § 6-22-104, you may cancel a residential roofing contract within 72 hours of signing — **and** within 72 hours of receiving written notice that your insurer denied the claim in whole or in part. The contractor must return any deposit within 10 days.
- **Your insurer can't unreasonably delay or deny a valid claim.** Under Colorado's prompt-payment statutes (C.R.S. § 10-3-1115 and § 10-3-1116), if an insurer delays or denies first-party benefits without a reasonable basis, you may recover **two times the covered benefit plus attorney fees and costs**. A roughly two-year window generally applies, so don't sit on a bad-faith dispute.

## What If Your Denver Claim Is Denied or Underpaid?

Don't accept it as final. In order:

1. **Request the written denial** with the specific reason.
2. **Have your contractor file a supplement** — the most common fix for an underpayment.
3. **Request a re-inspection** with your contractor present.
4. **Escalate** to a supervisor, file a complaint with the **Colorado Division of Insurance**, or bring in a **public adjuster**.
5. **If the insurer is acting in bad faith**, the prompt-payment statutes above (and a property-damage attorney) are your recourse.

## FAQ

**Who handles hail damage roof insurance claims in Denver?**
Your insurance company pays the claim, but the day-to-day work is handled by a licensed local roofing contractor with insurance-restoration experience — they inspect, document, meet the adjuster, file supplements, and complete the repair. Gates Enterprises does this for Denver-area homeowners and attends the adjuster meeting on every claim.

**Does homeowners insurance cover hail roof damage in Colorado?**
Almost always, yes. Colorado is one of the most hail-prone states in the country, and standard homeowner policies cover sudden hail and wind damage. You pay your deductible; insurance covers the rest of the approved scope.

**How long do I have to file a hail claim in Colorado?**
Most policies require "prompt" notice and set a filing window (often one to two years from the date of loss) — check your policy. File as soon as you've documented the damage; older damage is harder to attribute to a specific storm.

**Can my roofer pay my deductible?**
No. It's illegal in Colorado (C.R.S. § 6-22-105) and a sign of a contractor you should avoid.

**What does it cost to have a contractor handle my claim?**
Reputable insurance-restoration roofers, including Gates, handle the claim process — inspection, adjuster meeting, supplements — at no charge. Your only out-of-pocket cost on a covered claim is your deductible.

**What if the insurance estimate is lower than the repair cost?**
That's common and fixable. Your contractor files a supplement documenting the missed or underpriced items. A lower initial estimate is the starting point, not the final word.
